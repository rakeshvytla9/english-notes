### TOPIC 1: Uses of the Pronoun 'It'

#### Concept 1: 'It' for Singular Non-Living Things, Animals, Insects, and Infants
- **Rule & Formula/Structure:**
  $$\text{Singular (Non-living / Animal / Insect / Infant)} \longrightarrow \text{Pronoun: } \mathbf{It} \quad (\text{Possessive Adjective: } \mathbf{Its})$$
  $$\text{Plural (Non-living / Animals / Insects / Infants)} \longrightarrow \text{Pronoun: } \mathbf{They} \quad (\text{Possessive Adjective: } \mathbf{Their})$$

- **Detailed Explanation:**
  - **Non-living objects:** Used strictly when referring to a singular inanimate object. If plural, replace with **they/them/their**.
  - **Animals & Insects:** Singular animals and insects take **it**. Plural animals or insects take **they/them/their**.
  - **Infants/Babies:** When the biological sex/gender of an infant or baby is unknown or not specified in the context, the neutral singular pronoun **it** (possessive **its**) is used.
    - If the gender is explicitly known (boy/girl, male/female), **he** or **she** is used accordingly.
    - When referring to babies in general or an unknown infant, **it** is grammatically mandatory.

- **Class Examples:**
  - *America is a developed country. It is a superpower.* (One country $\rightarrow$ singular $\rightarrow$ **it**)
  - *Sri Lanka and Pakistan are developing countries. They are facing internal disturbance.* (Two countries $\rightarrow$ plural $\rightarrow$ **they**)
  - *I have a horse. It gives me yeoman's service.* (Singular animal $\rightarrow$ **it**)
  - *The baby was crying. It was hungry.* (Infant with unspecified gender $\rightarrow$ **it**)
  - *An ant stung me. It was furious.* (Singular insect $\rightarrow$ **it**)

- **Exceptions & SSC Exam Traps:**
  - **Trap:** Confusing the pronoun **it** with **they** when dealing with collective bodies, single countries, or institutions.
  - **Trap:** Do not use personal pronouns (*he/she*) for animals or infants unless gender is explicitly specified or personified.
  - **Trap:** Confusing **its** (possessive adjective) with **it's** (contraction of *it is* or *it has*).

---

#### Concept 2: Introductory 'It' (Empty / Dummy 'It')
- **Rule & Formula/Structure:**
  $$\mathbf{It} + \text{be-verb (is/was)} + [\text{Time / Weather / Temperature / Distance / Natural Event / Day / Date / Month}]$$

- **Detailed Explanation:**
  - In normal usage, a personal pronoun replaces a preceding noun (antecedent).
    - Example: *I have a dog. It is furious.* (Here, **it** is a true personal pronoun replacing *dog*).
  - However, when referring to time, weather, temperature, distance, or natural phenomena, English sentences require a formal subject even when there is no real actor or entity.
  - In such cases, **it** has no concrete semantic meaning of its own; it merely introduces the predicate. This is known as **Introductory It**, **Dummy It**, or **Empty It**.

- **Class Examples:**
  - *It is raining in Delhi.* (Weather / Natural event $\rightarrow$ Empty/Dummy **It**)
  - *It is winter now.* (Season / Natural event $\rightarrow$ Empty/Dummy **It**)
  - *It's February now.* [It's = It is] (Month $\rightarrow$ Empty/Dummy **It**)
  - *It is 11:00 AM / It is 5:00 PM.* (Time $\rightarrow$ Empty/Dummy **It**)
  - *It is Friday today.* (Day $\rightarrow$ Empty/Dummy **It**)

---

#### Concept 3: Pronoun Agreement for Institutions, Banks, Organizations & Mergers
- **Rule & Formula/Structure:**
  $$\text{Singular Organization / Board / Institution} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Its} \quad (\text{Possessive Adjective})$$
  $$\text{Plural Organizations / Multiple Companies} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Their} \quad (\text{Possessive Adjective})$$

- **Detailed Explanation:**
  - A singular organization, political party, corporate body, or institution (e.g., *PNB, SEBI, the ruling party, the college, India*) functions as a singular neuter entity. Any possessive reference back to it must be **its**, not **their**.
  - If multiple entities are joined (e.g., *a merger between two companies*) or if plural institutions are mentioned (e.g., *insurance companies, banks*), any possessive modifier referring to them must be **their**.
  - **Core Analytical Principle:** Always trace *whose* action or possession is being described:
    - In *The college is holding special lectures for its students...*, the students belong to **the college** (singular $\rightarrow$ **its**).
    - In *SEBI issued notices to companies seeking their explanation...*, the explanation is demanded from **the companies** (plural $\rightarrow$ **their**), not from SEBI.

### TOPIC 2: Introduction to Relative Pronouns

#### Concept 1: Nature and Dual Function of Relative Pronouns
- **Rule & Formula/Structure:**
  $$\text{Antecedent (Noun/Pronoun)} + [\mathbf{Relative\ Pronoun}] + \text{Subordinate Clause}$$

- **Detailed Explanation:**
  - A **Relative Pronoun** refers back to a preceding noun or pronoun (called the **Antecedent**) and relates it to the subordinate clause that follows.
  - **The Dual Power of a Relative Pronoun:**
    1. **Conjunctive Power:** Like a conjunction, it joins two clauses together.
    2. **Pronominal / Relational Power:** Unlike an ordinary conjunction (which merely connects clauses without playing a case role), a relative pronoun takes on a grammatical case (Subject or Object) and actively shows the relationship between the antecedent and the subsequent clause.
  - Major Relative Pronouns: **Who**, **Whom**, **Which**, **That**, **Whose**.

- **Fundamental SSC Rule — Constraint on Single Clauses:**
  $$\text{Single Clause } (1\text{ Subject } + 1\text{ Finite Verb}) \centernot\longrightarrow \text{Relative Pronoun}$$
  - A relative pronoun can **never** be used in a single clause. It requires at least two clauses to connect and relate.
  - *Incorrect:* I met an old man whom in the market. (Single clause)
  - *Correct:* I met an old man in the market. (1 Clause: Subject = *I*, Verb = *met*, Object = *an old man*)
  - *Correct:* He is the old man **whom** I met in the market. (2 Clauses: Clause 1 = *He is the old man*; Clause 2 = *I met [him] in the market*)

### TOPIC 3: Who vs. Whom

#### Concept 1: Traditional Structural Approach
- **Rule & Formula/Structure:**
  $$\text{Person (Antecedent)} + \mathbf{WHO} + \text{Finite Verb} \quad (\text{Subjective Case})$$
  $$\text{Person (Antecedent)} + \mathbf{WHOM} + \text{Subject} + \text{Finite Verb} \quad (\text{Objective Case})$$

- **Teacher's Explanation:**
  - Both **who** and **whom** strictly take a **person** as their antecedent.
  - **Who** functions as a **Subject Pronoun**. It is required when the subsequent clause lacks a subject for its finite verb.
  - **Whom** functions as an **Object Pronoun**. It is required when the subsequent clause already has its own subject and verb, and needs an object.

- **Class Demonstration Examples:**
  - *I know Rakhi ___ teaches English in Delhi Public School.*
    - Clause 1: Subject (*I*) + Verb (*know*) + Object (*Rakhi*).
    - Clause 2: Verb (*teaches*) + Object (*English*). The verb *teaches* needs a subject.
    - Since Rakhi is the person performing the action of teaching, the relative pronoun must act as a subject $\rightarrow$ **who**.
  - *I saw a girl ___ I know well.*
    - Clause 1: Subject (*I*) + Verb (*saw*) + Object (*a girl*).
    - Clause 2: Subject (*I*) + Verb (*know*) + Adverb (*well*).
    - The verb *know* already has its subject (*I*). The clause needs an object (*I know [her] well*).
    - To supply the object for Clause 2 while connecting it to *a girl* $\rightarrow$ **whom**.

---

#### Concept 2: The SSC Exam Trap in Complex Sentences
- Look at this classic exam sentence:
  - *Mr. Sharma is the teacher ___ we respect a lot teaches extremely well.*
- **The Visual Trap:**
  - An aspirant looking only immediately after the blank sees *we* (Subject) and *respect* (Verb).
  - Applying the simplistic rule ("Subject + Verb follows $\rightarrow$ use Whom"), 80% to 90% of candidates choose **whom**, which is **completely incorrect**.
- **The Hidden Finite Verb:**
  - Further down the sentence sits another finite verb: *teaches*.
  - *Mr. Sharma* pairs with *is*.
  - *We* pairs with *respect*.
  - What is the subject for the verb *teaches*? It has none!
  - Therefore, the blank must supply the subject for *teaches* $\rightarrow$ The correct answer is **who**!

---

#### Concept 3: The "Rafale Method" (Subject-Verb Balancing System)
To eliminate every possibility of error in complex sentences containing parenthetical clauses (e.g., *we think, I believe, I know, they say*), the teacher established the **Rafale Method**:

- **Core Rule:**
  1. Count all **Subjects** ($S$) in the sentence.
  2. Count all **Finite / Main Verbs** ($V$) in the sentence.
  3. Compare the totals:

$$\text{If } \mathbf{Verbs\ (V) > Subjects\ (S)} \implies \text{Use } \mathbf{WHO} \quad (\text{Supplies the missing Subject: } S + 1 = V)$$
$$\text{If } \mathbf{Verbs\ (V) == Subjects\ (S)} \implies \text{Use } \mathbf{WHOM} \quad (\text{Equation is already balanced; Object needed})$$

- **Critical Rules for Counting Verbs:**
  - **Auxiliary Verb + Main Verb = 1 Verb:** A verb phrase containing an auxiliary and a main verb counts as **one single finite verb unit** (e.g., *has helped* = 1 verb; *is trusted* = 1 verb; *will guide* = 1 verb).
  - **Standalone 'Be' Verbs = 1 Verb:** When *is, are, am, was, were* appear alone without another main verb, they serve as the main linking verb and count as **one verb** (e.g., *Mr. Sharma is...* $\rightarrow$ *is* = 1 verb).
  - **Non-Finite Verbs DO NOT Count:**
    - Infinitives ($to + V_1$) $\rightarrow$ **Do not count**.
    - Gerunds ($V_1 + \text{ing}$ functioning as a noun) $\rightarrow$ **Do not count**.
    - Participles functioning as adjectives $\rightarrow$ **Do not count**.

## 3. Class Practice Questions & Error Spotting

### Section A: Uses of 'It', 'Its', and 'Their'

#### Question 1
- **Sentence:** *America is a developed country. It is a superpower.*
- **Correct Pronoun:** **It**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** *America* is a single country (singular entity). Singular non-living entities/nations take the neutral singular personal pronoun **it**.

#### Question 2
- **Sentence:** *Sri Lanka & Pakistan are developing countries, It/they are facing internal disturbance.*
- **Answer:** **they**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** *Sri Lanka & Pakistan* constitutes a compound subject referring to two countries (plural). The plural pronoun for non-living/entities is **they**, not *it*.

#### Question 3
- **Sentence:** *I've a horse. It gives me yeomen's service.*
- **Correct Pronoun:** **It**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** *A horse* is a singular animal. Animals without specified gender take **it**.

#### Question 4
- **Sentence:** *The baby was crying, It was hungry.*
- **Correct Pronoun:** **It**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** *The baby* is an infant whose biological sex is not indicated in the sentence. An infant of unspecified sex takes the singular pronoun **it**.

#### Question 5
- **Sentence:** *An ant stung me. It was furious.*
- **Correct Pronoun:** **It**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** *An ant* is a singular insect. Insects take **it**.

#### Question 6
- **Sentence:** *The ruling party stood for the implementation of the bill and was ready to stake their/its political existence.*
- **Answer:** **its**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** *The ruling party* is a single political organization (singular noun). A singular organization takes the possessive adjective **its**, not *their*.

#### Question 7
- **Sentence:** *The only option for the PNB is to modernise their/its operations at all the branches.*
- **Answer:** **its**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** *PNB* (Punjab National Bank) is a single banking institution. Its possessive adjective must be singular: **its operations**, not *their*.

#### Question 8
- **Sentence:** *The ongoing merger between the 2 companies will have an adverse effect on its / their customers.*
- **Answer:** **their**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** The customers belong to the *two companies* undergoing the merger. Because *two companies* is plural, the possessive adjective must be **their customers**.

#### Question 9
- **Sentence:** *SEBI has recently issued notices to some insurance companies seeking its/ their explanation.*
- **Answer:** **their**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** Trace the logical possessor: SEBI is demanding explanations *from the insurance companies*. *Companies* is plural; therefore, the explanation belongs to them $\rightarrow$ **their explanation**.

#### Question 10
- **Sentence:** *The banks which do not meet its/ their targets are required to pay high penalties.*
- **Answer:** **their**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** The antecedent is *the banks* (plural noun). Plural nouns take the plural possessive adjective **their targets**.

#### Question 11
- **Sentence:** *The college is holding special lectures for its /their students & teachers, so it/they may get enlightened about the economic problems.*
- **Answer:** Blank 1: **its** | Blank 2: **they**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:**
  - Blank 1: The students and teachers belong to *the college* (singular institution) $\rightarrow$ **its students & teachers**.
  - Blank 2: The ones getting enlightened are the *students & teachers* (plural people) $\rightarrow$ **they may get enlightened**.

#### Question 12
- **Sentence:** *India began their/its nuclear energy programme during the late fifties.*
- **Answer:** **its**
- **Grammar Rule Applied:** *India* refers to a single nation/state. As a singular entity, its possessive form is **its**, not *their*.

### Section B: Relative Pronouns (Who vs. Whom)

#### Question 13
- **Sentence:** *I have two sons ___ are careless.*
- **Answer:** **who**
- **Analysis (Rafale Method):**
  - $S_1$: *I*, $V_1$: *have*
  - $V_2$: *are*
  - Subjects = 1, Verbs = 2.
  - Verbs (2) > Subjects (1) $\implies$ Needs a subject $\rightarrow$ **who**.

#### Question 14
- **Sentence:** *I saw an old man ___ was blind in his eyes.*
- **Answer:** **who**
- **Analysis (Rafale Method):**
  - $S_1$: *I*, $V_1$: *saw*
  - $V_2$: *was*
  - Subjects = 1, Verbs = 2.
  - Verbs (2) > Subjects (1) $\implies$ Needs a subject $\rightarrow$ **who**.

#### Question 15
- **Sentence:** *I met a boy ___ I taught.*
- **Answer:** **whom**
- **Analysis (Rafale Method):**
  - $S_1$: *I*, $V_1$: *met*
  - $S_2$: *I*, $V_2$: *taught*
  - Subjects = 2, Verbs = 2.
  - Subjects (2) == Verbs (2) $\implies$ Balanced; needs an object $\rightarrow$ **whom**.

#### Question 16
- **Sentence:** *Mr. Sharma is the teacher ___ we respect a lot teaches extremely well.*
- **Answer:** **who**
- **Analysis (Rafale Method):**
  - $S_1$: *Mr. Sharma*, $V_1$: *is*
  - $S_2$: *we*, $V_2$: *respect*
  - $V_3$: *teaches*
  - Total Subjects = 2 (*Mr. Sharma, we*)
  - Total Verbs = 3 (*is, respect, teaches*)
  - Verbs (3) > Subjects (2) $\implies$ Shortage of 1 subject $\rightarrow$ **who**.

#### Question 17
- **Sentence:** *He is the man ___ I know very well, has helped my son in the final examinations.*
- **Answer:** **who**
- **Analysis (Rafale Method):**
  - $S_1$: *He*, $V_1$: *is*
  - $S_2$: *I*, $V_2$: *know*
  - $V_3$: *has helped* (Auxiliary *has* + Main verb *helped* = 1 finite verb unit)
  - Total Subjects = 2 (*He, I*)
  - Total Verbs = 3 (*is, know, has helped*)
  - Verbs (3) > Subjects (2) $\implies$ Needs a subject $\rightarrow$ **who**.

#### Question 18
- **Sentence:** *Ram Singh is certainly an inspector ___ I know very well is trustworthy beyond doubt and meticulous in his habit.*
- **Answer:** **who**
- **Analysis (Rafale Method):**
  - $S_1$: *Ram Singh*, $V_1$: *is*
  - $S_2$: *I*, $V_2$: *know*
  - $V_3$: *is*
  - Total Subjects = 2 (*Ram Singh, I*)
  - Total Verbs = 3 (*is, know, is*)
  - Verbs (3) > Subjects (2) $\implies$ Needs a subject $\rightarrow$ **who**.

#### Question 19
- **Sentence:** *Mr. Sharma is a person ___ lives in Dehradun, wants to help the needy.*
- **Answer:** **who**
- **Analysis (Rafale Method):**
  - $S_1$: *Mr. Sharma*, $V_1$: *is*
  - $V_2$: *lives*
  - $V_3$: *wants*
  - Note: *to help* is an infinitive ($to + V_1$) and is **not counted**.
  - Total Subjects = 1 (*Mr. Sharma*), Total Verbs = 3 (*is, lives, wants*)
  - Verbs > Subjects $\implies$ **who**.

#### Question 20
- **Sentence:** *This is the teacher ___ taught my children.*
- **Answer:** **who**
- **Analysis (Rafale Method):**
  - $S_1$: *This*, $V_1$: *is*
  - $V_2$: *taught*
  - Total Subjects = 1, Total Verbs = 2.
  - Verbs (2) > Subjects (1) $\implies$ **who**.

#### Question 21
- **Sentence:** *The person ___ I like most is my mother.*
- **Answer:** **whom**
- **Analysis (Rafale Method):**
  - $S_1$: *The person*, $V_1$: *is*
  - $S_2$: *I*, $V_2$: *like*

  - Total Verbs = 2 (*is, like*)
  - Subjects (2) == Verbs (2) $\implies$ Balanced $\rightarrow$ **whom**.

## 4. Teacher's Exam Shortcuts & Common Pitfalls

### The "Rafale Method" Execution Shortcut
1. When you see a fill-in-the-blank or error-spotting question involving **who vs. whom**:
   - Do **NOT** rely on how the sentence sounds.
   - Do **NOT** merely look at the single word following the blank.
2. Label each grammatical subject: $S_1, S_2, S_3\dots$
3. Label each finite verb: $V_1, V_2, V_3\dots$
   - Treat $[ \text{has/have/had/is/was/will} + V_{\text{main}} ]$ as a single verb unit.
   - Cross out any non-finite verb forms ($to + V_1$, gerunds) so they do not distort your count.
4. Compare your totals:
   - If $V > S \implies$ Fill **WHO** immediately.
   - If $V == S \implies$ Fill **WHOM** immediately.

### Critical Exam Pitfalls
- **The Parenthetical Clause Illusion:**
  - Sentences containing clauses like *I think*, *we believe*, *I know*, or *they say* are specifically designed by SSC examiners to trick you into choosing *whom*.
  - *Example:* *The man [who / whom] I think is honest.*
    - $S_1$: *The man*, $S_2$: *I*
    - $V_1$: *think*, $V_2$: *is*
    - $V$ (2) > $S$ (1) without the relative pronoun $\implies$ The answer is **who**, not *whom*.
- **The Single-Clause Illusion:**
  - Examiners sometimes insert *whom* or *who* into a single-clause sentence to create an error.
  - A relative pronoun cannot exist without at least two distinct finite verbs connecting two clauses.
- **Possessive Agreement with Organizations:**
  - Aspirants often think of the individuals inside a company or college and erroneously write *their*.
  - Unless the noun is formally plural (*colleges, banks, companies*), an institution is singular neuter and requires **its**.

### Vocabulary & Idioms Highlighted in Class
- **Yeoman's service:** Excellent, reliable, and substantial assistance/work.
  - *Example:* *I have a horse. It gives me yeoman's service.*
- **Furious:** Extremely fierce, violent, or aggressive.
  - *Example:* *An ant stung me. It was furious.*
- **Meticulous:** Showing immense, thorough care and precision regarding details.
  - *Example:* *Ram Singh is certainly an inspector... meticulous in his habit.*
- **Heart and soul:** Completely, wholeheartedly, with one's entire devotion.
  - *Example:* *This is the girl whom I love heart and soul.*
- **Posterity:** Future generations; people of the future.
  - *Example:* *Preserve these articles for posterity.*
- **Call on (Phrasal Verb):** To visit someone (usually briefly or formally).
  - *Example:* *...called on me last night.*

```
                                QUICK REVISION CHEAT SHEET
========================================================================================

1. PRONOUN 'IT'
   • Singular Non-living / Animals / Insects / Unknown-gender Infants -> IT / ITS
   • Plural of all the above                                          -> THEY / THEIR
   • Empty / Dummy 'It' -> Used as formal subject for:
     Time, Weather, Temperature, Distance, Day, Date, Month
     (e.g., "It is raining", "It is 11 AM", "It is Friday")

2. INSTITUTIONAL PRONOUN AGREEMENT
   • Singular Institution (PNB, SEBI, College, India, Party) -> ITS
   • Plural Institutions (Banks, Companies, Parties)         -> THEIR
   • Always identify WHO owns the object (SEBI vs. Companies).

3. RELATIVE PRONOUNS — FUNDAMENTALS
   • Relative Pronoun = Conjunction (joins clauses) + Pronoun (shows case/relation)
   • Strict Rule: CANNOT be used in a single clause (Requires >= 2 clauses).
   • Antecedent for WHO/WHOM must be a PERSON.

4. RAFALE METHOD: WHO vs. WHOM
   Step 1: Count Subjects (S).
   Step 2: Count Finite Verbs (V).
           - Helping Verb + Main Verb = 1 Verb
           - Do NOT count non-finites (to + V1, Gerunds)
   Step 3: Evaluate:
           • If Verbs > Subjects (V > S)   ===>  WHO   (Acts as Subject)
           • If Verbs == Subjects (V == S) ===>  WHOM  (Acts as Object)
========================================================================================
```
