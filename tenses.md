# 📚 The Master Guide: Present Tenses & Quiz Breakdown
*A complete structural breakdown of the Present Continuous, Present Perfect Continuous, and Present Perfect Simple, followed by an exhaustive quiz review.*

---

## 1. PRESENT CONTINUOUS (The "Right Now")
**Core Concept:** Actions happening exactly at the moment of speaking, or temporary ongoing situations.

### A. Structure
* **Affirmative:** Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing
* **Negative:** Subject + am/is/are + not + verb-ing
* **Question:** Am/Is/Are + Subject + verb-ing?
* *Spelling Rules:* Drop silent 'e' (making); Change 'ie' to 'y' (lying); Double last consonant for short CVC words (running).

### B. When to Use It
1. **Happening right now:** *I am typing.* (Keywords: now, right now, currently)
2. **Temporary periods:** *She is studying French this semester.* (Keywords: these days, this month)
3. **Changing/Developing situations:** *The internet is getting faster.* (Keywords: getting, becoming)
4. **Definite future plans:** *We are flying to Tokyo tomorrow.* (Keywords: tomorrow, next week)
5. **Annoying habits:** *He is always interrupting me!* (Must be paired with "always").

### C. 🚨 THE EXCEPTIONS
**Exception 1: Stative Verbs (The "No -ING" Rule)**
Verbs describing a state of being, emotion, sense, or possession cannot be used in this tense, even if the feeling is happening *right now*.
* *Wrong:* I am wanting coffee. / I am knowing the answer.
* *Right:* I **want** coffee. / I **know** the answer.

**Exception 2: "Mixed" Verbs**
Some verbs act as both stative and action verbs.
* **Think:** Opinion (Stative) = *I think it's bad.* | Mental Process (Action) = *I am thinking.*
* **Have:** Possession (Stative) = *I have a car.* | Experiencing (Action) = *I am having lunch.*

---

## 2. PRESENT PERFECT CONTINUOUS (The "Process / Duration")
**Core Concept:** The bridge between past and present. Focuses on *how long* an action has been happening.

### A. Structure
* **Affirmative:** Subject + has/have + been + verb-ing
* **Negative:** Subject + has/have + not + been + verb-ing
* **Question:** Has/Have + Subject + been + verb-ing?

### B. When to Use It
1. **Unfinished Actions (Focus on Duration):** Started in the past and still happening now.
  * *Example:* "I have been waiting for 45 minutes." (Keywords: for, since, all day)
2. **Recent Actions with Present Physical Evidence:** The process just stopped, but you can see the result.
  * *Example:* "You are sweating! Have you been running?"

### C. 🚨 THE EXCEPTIONS
**Exception 1: Stative Verbs Return**
You cannot use stative verbs in this tense either, even to express duration. Drop back to the Perfect Simple.
* *Wrong:* "I have been knowing him for ten years."
* *Right:* "I **have known** him for ten years."

**Exception 2: The "Live & Work" Pass**
For the verbs **live** and **work**, you can use either Perfect Simple or Perfect Continuous to express duration. Both mean the exact same thing.
* "I **have lived** here for five years." = "I **have been living** here for five years."

---

## 3. PRESENT PERFECT SIMPLE (The "Experience / Result")
**Core Concept:** An action completed in the past that has a direct, important connection to the present.

### A. Structure
* **Affirmative:** Subject + has/have + Past Participle (V3)
* **Negative:** Subject + has/have + not + Past Participle (V3)
* **Question:** Has/Have + Subject + Past Participle (V3)?

### B. When to Use It
1. **Life Experiences:** Things done in your life up to now. (*I have visited Japan three times.*)
2. **Recent Actions with a Present Result:** A sudden past action causing a current situation. (*I have lost my keys.* -> Now I am locked out).
3. **Accomplishments up to Now:** (*I have drunk three coffees today.*)

### C. 🚨 THE EXCEPTIONS & NUANCES
**Exception 1: "Been" vs. "Gone"**
* **Has/Have gone:** Traveled somewhere and **is still there**. (*She has gone to Paris.*)
* **Has/Have been:** Traveled somewhere and **has returned home**. (*She has been to Paris.*)

**Nuance: The "Specific Time" Trap**
If you state exactly *when* an action happened (yesterday, last year, in 2010), you CANNOT use the Present Perfect. You must use the Simple Past.
* *Wrong:* "I have seen that movie yesterday."
* *Right:* "I **saw** that movie yesterday."

## 4. COMPREHENSIVE QUIZ REVIEW & EXPLANATIONS

**1. I ______ for the bus for 45 minutes, and it still hasn't arrived!**
* **Correct: have been waiting**. Captures an ongoing action + duration.
* *Wrong:* "am waiting" (cannot be used with duration); "have waited" (implies the waiting is completely over); "wait" (used for general habits).

**2. We are best friends. I ______ her since we were in kindergarten.**
* **Correct: have known**. 'Know' is a stative verb and cannot take an "-ing". 
* *Wrong:* "have been knowing" / "am knowing" (stative verbs can't use continuous form); "know" (ignores the past connection 'since').

**3. Shh! Please be quiet. The baby ______ in the next room right now.**
* **Correct: is sleeping**. Action happening temporarily at this exact second.
* *Wrong:* "has been sleeping" (needs a duration marker like 'for 2 hours'); "has slept" (means the sleep is finished); "sleeps" (used for general routine).

**4. I ______ to Paris three times in my life, and I'd love to go again.**
* **Correct: have been**. The rule for traveling somewhere and returning.
* *Wrong:* "have gone" (means you are still in Paris right now); "have been going" (implies one massive continuous trip); "am going" (implies a future plan or currently on a plane).

**5. I ______ the book you lent me, but I'm only on chapter 3 so I haven't finished it yet.**
* **Correct: have been reading**. Perfect for an unfinished process that you are currently in the middle of.
* *Wrong:* "have read" (means you finished the whole book); "am reading" (ignores the past connection of borrowing it); "read" (simple past implies it's totally finished).

**6. I can't get into my house because I ______ my keys.**
* **Correct: have lost**. A sudden past action causing an immediate, highly visible present result.
* *Wrong:* "have been losing" / "am losing" (losing keys is a sudden event, not a continuous process you do over hours); "lose" (implies a daily habit).

**7. My roommate ______ his dirty dishes in the sink. It's so frustrating!**
* **Correct: is always leaving**. Idiom: 'always' + Present Continuous expresses an excessively annoying habit.
* *Wrong:* "has always left" (states a dry historical fact without the emotional frustration); "always leave" (grammatically incorrect for singular roommate); "has been always leaving" (awkward structure).

**8. I can't talk on the phone right now, I ______ lunch with my boss.**
* **Correct: am having**. 'Have' acts as a dynamic verb here (meaning eating/experiencing), happening right now.
* *Wrong:* "have" (implies possession or daily habit); "have had" (means lunch is finished, so you *could* talk); "have been having" (focuses unnecessarily on duration instead of the immediate action).

**9. You look completely exhausted and you are sweating! ______?**
* **Correct: Have you been running?** The running process recently stopped, but the physical evidence (sweat) is highly visible right now.
* *Wrong:* "Are you running?" (they aren't running right this second); "Have you run?" (asks about life experience, not immediate evidence); "Do you run?" (asks about general hobbies).

**10. She ______ at this company for ten years, and she still loves her daily tasks.**
* **Correct: has been working**. Focuses on the active duration of the verb 'work'. ('Has worked' is also correct, but emphasizes permanence).
* *Wrong:* "is working" (cannot be used with 'for ten years'); "works" (cannot be used with duration markers in English); "am working" (wrong subject-verb agreement).
